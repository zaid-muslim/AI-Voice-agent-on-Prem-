# HBL Islamic Banking (HBL Sadiq)

## Overview

HBL is one of the largest Islamic banking players in Pakistan, offering Shariah-compliant
banking under its Islamic banking brand. All Islamic products are approved by HBL's own Shariah
Board, made up of recognized Shariah scholars.

## Islamic Accounts

### Islamic Saving Accounts
Several Shariah-compliant saving account options are available, including the HBL Islamic PLS
Account, HBL Islamic Uraan Account, and HBL Islamic Mahana Amdan Account. These are structured
around Islamic finance concepts rather than conventional interest.

### Islamic Term Deposit Accounts
Based on the Mudaraba principle: the customer (Rab-ul-Maal) provides funds to HBL (Mudarib) for
Shariah-compliant investment. Actual profit earned at the end of the deposit period is shared
between the customer and the bank according to a pre-agreed Profit Sharing Ratio (PSR), rather
than a fixed interest rate — the exact return is not guaranteed in advance the way conventional
interest is.

### Islamic Current Accounts
Allow customers to deposit, hold, and withdraw funds in a Shariah-compliant structure, with no
profit paid (consistent with conventional current accounts).

### Islamic Asaan Account
A Shariah-compliant equivalent of the conventional HBL Asaan Account, aimed at easy account
opening for new-to-bank customers.

## Islamic Financing

HBL offers Shariah-compliant financing products, including Islamic Consumer/Personal Financing,
through its dedicated Islamic Banking branches and Islamic banking windows within conventional
branches. These are structured as Shariah-compliant alternatives to conventional personal loans
(e.g. Murabaha or similar Islamic financing structures) rather than interest-based lending.

## Important Note for Callers

Islamic banking products use profit-sharing and Shariah-compliant structures rather than
interest/markup — do not describe an Islamic account's return as a fixed "interest rate"; refer
to it as an expected profit rate or profit-sharing ratio, and clarify that actual amounts can vary
depending on the underlying Shariah structure.
